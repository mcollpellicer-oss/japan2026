DAY 1

Friday, Oct 23
🎯 DAY 1 TOP TIPS & STRATEGY
  • Bus-Free Arrival: Drop bags at M's Hotel Nijojo Oike and walk 3 minutes to Nijojo-mae Station. Take the Tozai Subway Line straight to Keage Station—zero bus traffic.
  • First Night Illuminations: Friday kicks off Kyoto's Autumn Night Viewings. Kiyomizu-dera and Kodai-ji light up after dusk; do not skip the evening walk!

11:30 AM Arrival & Hotel Drop

Arrive from Kanazawa via the direct JR Thunderbird Limited Express (~2 hours). Transfer to the Karasuma Subway Line to Karasuma-Oike, switch 1 stop on the Tozai Line to Nijojo-mae Station. Drop bags at M's Hotel Nijojo Oike.

01:30 PM Northern Canal & Philosopher's Path Walk

Take the Tozai Line directly from Nijojo-mae to Keage Station. Walk through the massive wooden Sanmon Gate at Nanzen-ji, inspect the 1890s Western brick Suirokaku Aqueduct, then follow the quiet canal path north past the moss-covered gate of Honen-in to Ginkaku-ji (Silver Pavilion).

💡 Cultural Context: Philosopher's Path & Nanzen-ji

Philosopher's Path: Named after Nishida Kitaro, one of Japan's most prominent 20th-century philosophers, who practiced walking meditation along this stone canal daily on his route to Kyoto University.

Suirokaku Aqueduct: Built in 1890 as part of the Lake Biwa Canal project to modernize Kyoto. Constructing a Western Roman-style red-brick aqueduct right through a 13th-century Zen temple was controversial, creating one of Kyoto's most striking architectural contrasts.

Ginkaku-ji (Silver Pavilion): Built in 1482 by Shogun Ashikaga Yoshimasa as a retirement villa. Unlike the Golden Pavilion, it was never covered in silver leaf due to war interruptions. It embodies Wabi-sabi—the deep aesthetic appreciation of simplicity, restraint, and understated elegance.

📍 Open Day 1 Afternoon Map (Subway + Philosopher's Path)

05:30 PM Southern Higashiyama Autumn Night Illuminations

Stroll through the preserved lantern-lit stone alleys of Ninenzaka & Sannenzaka. Visit Kiyomizu-dera to experience the pitch-black Tainai-meguri tunnel beneath the hall and view the temple stage lit against the night sky, followed by the glowing moss gardens at Kodai-ji.

💡 Cultural Context: Kiyomizu-dera & Tainai-meguri

Kiyomizu-dera: Founded in 778 AD, famous for its massive wooden stage built entirely without nails, jutting out over the hillside overlooking Kyoto.

Tainai-meguri: Translates to "entering the womb." You navigate a completely pitch-black basement beneath the Zuigudo hall in total silence holding a single beaded handrail, symbolizing spiritual rebirth as you re-emerge into the light.

DAY 2

Saturday, Oct 24
🎯 DAY 2 TOP TIPS & STRATEGY
  • Strict 07:00 AM Departure: Fushimi Inari gets swarmed by tour groups by 09:15 AM. Hitting the red torii gates at 7:00 AM guarantees quiet photos and peaceful mountain walking.
  • Direct Southbound Rail: Fushimi Inari, Uji, and Nara sit on the exact same JR train line. Zero transfers required between stops.

07:00 AM Fushimi Inari-taisha

From M's Hotel, take the Tozai Subway 2 stops to Sanjo-Keihan and transfer to the Keihan Main Line down to Fushimi-Inari Station. Walk through the Senbon Torii (thousands of vermilion gates) up to the Yotsutsuji intersection for city panoramas. Take a break at Inari Saryo on the descent.

💡 Cultural Context: Fushimi Inari & Fox Messengers

Fushimi Inari: The head shrine for Inari, the Shinto deity of rice, agriculture, and business prosperity. The thousands of torii gates are all individual donations from Japanese businesses, with the donor's company name and date carved into the back of each wood post.

Kitsune (Foxes): Stone fox statues guard the shrine carrying keys, scrolls, or rice sheaves in their mouths. They act as sacred messengers for Inari rather than deities themselves.

10:00 AM Uji Matcha Capital

Hop on the southbound JR Nara Line to Uji Station. Visit Byodo-in Temple (Phoenix Hall) on its reflection pond and cross the river to Ujigami-jinja (Japan's oldest standing Shinto shrine).

💡 Cultural Context: Byodo-in & Uji Matcha

Byodo-in: Built in 1053, its Phoenix Hall (*Hoo-do*) represents Pure Land Buddhist heavenly paradise on earth. It is so famous it is stamped onto the Japanese 10-yen coin.

Uji Matcha: Uji's misty river climate produced Japan's first cultivated green tea in the 12th century, remaining the premier standard for ceremonial matcha nationwide.

01:30 PM Wonders of Nara Walking Tour

Continue south on the JR line to Nara Station. Execute the 5.3km walking route: Yoshikien Garden ➔ Todai-ji (Daibutsu Great Buddha) ➔ Nigatsu-do views ➔ forest trail to Kasuga Taisha shrine ➔ Naramachi district.

💡 Cultural Context: Todai-ji & Sacred Deer

Todai-ji: Constructed in 752 AD, housing the world's largest bronze Buddha statue (*Daibutsu*). The Great Buddha Hall remains one of the largest wooden structures on earth.

Nara Deer: According to local Shinto legend, the god of Kasuga Taisha arrived in Nara riding a sacred white deer. Today, over 1,200 free-roaming deer are protected as living national treasures.

🚇 Transit Tip: From M's Hotel, walk 3 mins to Nijojo-mae, take the Tozai line to Sanjo-Keihan, then Keihan line straight to Fushimi Inari. From Fushimi Inari, use the JR Line for Uji and Nara.

🍽️ Day 2 Meal & Beverage Plan

  • Early Breakfast: High-quality convenience store onigiri and hot green tea grabbed the night before to eat before your 7:00 AM start.
  • Mid-Morning Tea (Uji): Tsuen Tea (the world's oldest operating tea house, operating since 1160 AD) or Tsujiri Uji for rich matcha parfaits.
  • Afternoon Sweets Break (Nara): Mizuya-chaya (a thatched-roof tea house nestled deep in the forest along the Kasuga Taisha trail).
  • Late Lunch / Early Dinner (Nara): Kuruminoki Shikanofune Kamado (Naramachi slow-food cooked in traditional wood-fired ovens).
  • Dinner Backup: Hiraso (famous for kakinoha-zushi — sushi wrapped in persimmon leaves).
  • Evening Sake Flight (Kyoto): Fushimi Sake Village (Kura Koji) for an 18-sake tasting flight, or Torisei for draft sake inside an authentic brewery.

DAY 3

Sunday, Oct 25
🎯 DAY 3 TOP TIPS & STRATEGY
  • 07:30 AM Arashiyama Arrival: Beat the tour buses to the Bamboo Grove. Head straight into Okochi Sanso Villa the moment gates open at 8:30 AM.
  • The 25th Flea Market: Today is Sunday, Oct 25! The monthly Tenjin-san Flea Market at Kitano Tenman-gu is a top highlight for antique ceramics, kimonos, and food stalls.
  • Randen Streetcar Shortcut: Ride the retro Randen tram directly between Arashiyama and Kitano Tenman-gu to glide past road traffic.

07:30 AM Arashiyama Bamboo & Villa Gardens

Walk 8 minutes from M's Hotel to JR Nijo Station and take the train directly to Saga-Arashiyama Station (10 mins). Walk through the silent Bamboo Grove, enter Okochi Sanso Villa for matcha with gorge views, and pass through Tenryu-ji Garden down to Togetsu-kyo Bridge.

💡 Cultural Context: Okochi Sanso & Tenryu-ji

Okochi Sanso: The former private estate of silent-film samurai actor Denjiro Okochi, who spent 30 years creating these tranquil hilltop gardens overlooking the Arashiyama gorge.

Tenryu-ji: Rinzai Zen temple founded in 1339. Its 14th-century Sogenchi garden incorporates the Arashiyama mountains as a natural backdrop using the technique of Shakkei (borrowed scenery).

📍 Open Day 3 Morning Map (Arashiyama Route)

11:30 AM Tenjin-san Antique Market (Oct 25 Only)

Take the Keifuku Randen tram from Arashiyama to Kitano-Hakubaicho. Walk to Kitano Tenman-gu shrine for the famous monthly Tenjin-san Flea Market. Browse hundreds of outdoor stalls selling antique ceramics, vintage kimono fabrics, woodblock prints, and street food.

02:00 PM Northwest Zen Quick Hits

Surgical strike to Ryoan-ji to contemplate the famous Zen rock garden, followed by Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion). Take the iconic photo across the pond and leave promptly.

💡 Cultural Context: Ryoan-ji Rock Garden

Ryoan-ji: Contains 15 rocks arranged inside a rectangle of raked white gravel. The arrangement is designed so that from whichever angle you view the garden while seated on the veranda, at least one rock remains hidden from sight, symbolizing that human understanding is inherently incomplete.

05:30 PM Geiko District Twilight Stroll

Return to central Kyoto. Execute the self-guided evening walk: Yasaka-jinja ➔ historic dark-wood alleys of Hanami-koji ➔ Tatsumi-bashi bridge in Shimbashi ➔ across the river to lantern-lit Ponto-cho Alley and Kiyamachi-dori canal.

📍 Open Day 3 Evening Map (Geiko District Walk)
🚇 Transit Tip: Walk from M's Hotel to JR Nijo Station (8 min walk) for the fastest train to Arashiyama (10 mins). Use the Randen streetcar to travel from Arashiyama to Kitano Tenman-gu.

🍽️ Day 3 Meal & Beverage Plan

  • Morning Bakery / Coffee: Warewarewa Arashiyama (bakery in a 210-year-old thatched-roof machiya) or Sagano-yu (bathhouse cafe).
  • Lunch (Arashiyama): Shigetsu (Michelin-rated Zen Buddhist vegetarian cuisine inside Tenryu-ji) or exCafe Arashiyama (grill your own dango over charcoal).
  • Market Street Food / Snacks: Try fresh yakisoba, taiyaki, or dango skewers at the Tenjin-san Market, or tofu specialties at Toyouke-jaya across from the shrine.
  • Afternoon Sweets: Telacoya for hanami dango skewers along the riverbank.
  • Dinner: Relaxed izakaya night along the Ponto-cho lanterns or Kiyamachi canal. Options: Gion Yuki, Teppan Tavern Tenamonya, or Ochazuke Bar & Cafe Chi.
